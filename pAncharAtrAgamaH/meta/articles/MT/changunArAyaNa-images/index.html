The saMkarShaNa and vAsudeva in the ChangunArAyaNa style sculptures

The famous viShNu vishvarUpa at ChangunArAyaNa in Nepal, and its supposed cognate of unknown provenance, whose fate is currently unknown (please post if you know more of it or a higher-res image), present an interesting early pA~ncharAtrika tradition.

saMkarShaNa

The supine figure below on the serpentine bed is the saMkarShaNa: his association with the serpent and his holding the pestle (musala) clearly mark him out as such.

The four dig-gaja-s and the goddess pR^ithivI are above him, indicating that he is the substratrum to even the earth and its props.

Coils

This, along with the symbolism of the serpent’s coils, represents cyclical time in the language of myth – the sinusoidal coils of the snake are the periodicity of kAla emerging from the rotations of the earth – hence, time is shown as its substratrum. +++(5)+++

Axis viShNu

The axis of this rotation is also viShNu and is depicted by his form standing erect like a pillar above the saMkarShaNa. This role of viShNu is brought out well by the famous pUrvapIThikA of the dasha-kumAra-charita of daNDin of kA~nchipuram:

brahmANDa Chatra daNDaH
shata-dhR^iti bhavanAmbho-ruho nAladaNDaH kShoNI-naukUpa daNDaH kSharad-amara-sarit-paTTikA-ketu-daNDaH jyotish-chakrAkSha daNDas- tribhuvana vijayastambho .a~Nghri daNDaH shreyas-traivikramas te vitaratu vibudha dveSiNAM kAla daNDaH ||

The rod of the umbrella cap of the universe, the reed stock for the lotus house of him of 100 sacrifices, the mast rod of the ship that is the earth the rod for the banner that is the heavenly stream (the Milky Way), the axis of the rotating star-wheel, the rod that is the victory pillar of the 3 worlds, may the foot [of viShNu] favor you, the rod that is the leg of trivikrama with which he strode to conquer the 3 worlds, is the rod of time (death) to the enemies of the devas.

vAsudeva vishvarUpa

This upper viShNu is widely understood as viShNu vishvarUpa. The presence of arjuna (symmetrically with garuDa) also signals that he is the vAsudeva. Indeed, he is depicted in his para form emerging above the 9 heads in three tiers, transcending the triple world.

Further, this is made clear by the pradyumna and the aniruddha shown emerging from him (just beside his consorts shrI and hrI, equated with rukmiNI and satyabhAmA in the sAttvata flavor of the shAsana).

sankarShaNa-core

However, this depiction would also suggest that the vAsudeva manifests from the substratum of the saMkarShaNa (hinted in the sAttvata religion by his seniority). This hints at a tradition paralleled by the sanatkumAra saMhitA, wherein the saMkarShaNa is the primary vyUha who emanates the avatAra-s.+++(5)+++

This further warrants closer examination of the early pA~ncharAtrika texts preserved in Nepal in light of this iconography.

Serpent

Finally, this depiction, with the serpentine saMkarShaNa as the “mount” for the vAsudeva has deep roots in early vaiShNava tradition, perhaps in what may be considered the earliest expression of pA~ncharAtra or even the undifferentiated vaiShNava-shAsana. This is seen in the vAsiShTha-sarpa-vidyA khila-mantra-s in the R^igveda tradition (shAkala/Kashmirian RV khila):

kALiko nAma sarpo nava-nAga-sahasra-balaH | yamuna hrade ha so jAto .asau nArAyaNa-vAhanaH ||
The serpent named kALika (literally time), possessing the strength of nine thousand elephants – he was born indeed in the lake of the yamunA, this is the mount of nArAyana.

yadi kALika-dUtasya yadi kAH kALIkAd bhayaM |
janma-bhUmiM parikrAnto nirviSho yAti kALikaH ||
Whether there be fear from a messenger of kALika, or whatever [fear there be] from kALika himself, having turned back towards his birthplace, kALika departs poisonless.

These verses have the archaic version (kALika or kAlika, in some versions, like in some occurrences in the shA~NkhAyana version) of the name of kAliya, who is subdued by kR^iShNa devakIputra in the sAttvata cycle in the lake on the yamunA.

A version of this snake has also been appropriated by the bauddha-s (lalitavistAra, mahAvastu, ashvaghoSha’s buddhacharita), where he (kAla or kAlika) comes with his wife to praise the bodhisattva just before he attains enlightenment. Notably, he is called nArAyaNa-vAhana, indicating that he was originally the time-serpent mount of viShNu – something captured in the Nepalian images.

Hence, we propose that the sAttvata tradition, like with other things, “reverse-euhmerized” the old kAlika to present him as kAliya/kAli~Nga. Thus, the serpent of time, kAlika, as the mount or bed of viShNu, goes back to an early phase of the shAsana and came to be identified with the saMkarShaNa, who (as we noted before) also had his early serpentine connections.
