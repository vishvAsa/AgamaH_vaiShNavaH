ratnAkara, the great Kashmirian kavi, was a saiddhAntika shaiva, who, unlike the nishvAsa scripture of his tradition, took a perennialist stance. 

> praNavAdir apyaya-virAmatAM dadhat  
phaNinA nirUpita-padArtha-saptakaH |  
bhagavann adhIta iha yaiH kilAgamaH  
padam Apnuvanti nanu te .api tAvakam ||

> O lord, those who study that Agama (of 50 chapters) which starts with [the chapter on] praNava and comes to a conclusion with the chapter on dissolution, wherein the seven categories (bhagavad, vidyA, karman, kAla, kartavyatA, iti-kartavyatA and saMyama) are expounded by the snake (saMkarShaNa), they too surely attain your state.

In this verse from the haravijaya, he holds that even the pA~ncharAtrika-s who follow the saMkarShaNa-sUtra-s attain shiva. This is an allusion to a pA~ncharAtrika vaiShNava scripture of 50 chapters that has been lost in South India and Nepal (to my knowledge) that is said to have been expounded by the saMkarShaNa. However, we get glimpses of that tradition in other Agama-s like sanatkumAra.
