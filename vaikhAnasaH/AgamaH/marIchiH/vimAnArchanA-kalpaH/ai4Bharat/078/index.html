078: Pavitrāropaṇa-vidhiḥ

What's in this page?

(Rules for Sacred Thread Offerings)

This document contains the Sanskrit text, IAST transliteration, and English translation of the 78th Chapter (Aṣṭasaptatitamaḥ Paṭalaḥ) of the Vimānārcanākalpa (attributed to Sage Marīci, representing the Vaikhānasa school of Vaishnavism).

This chapter details the ritual procedures for pavitrāropaṇa (the offering of sacred threads) to the deity. It explains the purpose of the ritual, its timing, the preparation of the threads (lengths, knots, and materials), the purification and consecration on the eleventh day (ekādaśy-adhivāsana), the dedication of the threads on the twelfth day (dvādaśy-arpaṇa), and the concluding ceremonies.

Section 1: The Fruit and Necessity of Sacred Thread Offering

Sanskrit (Devanagari) & Transliteration (IAST)

अथ अष्टसप्ततितमः पटलः पवित्रारोपणम् atha aṣṭasaptatitamaḥ paṭalaḥ pavitrāropaṇam

अथ पवित्रारोपणं वक्ष्ये — पवित्रारोपणस्य फलम् atha pavitrāropaṇaṃ vakṣye — pavitrāropaṇasya phalam

सर्वयज्ञफलप्रदं, सर्वदोषोपशमनं, सर्वतुष्टिकरं, सर्वकामप्रदं, सर्वलोक-शान्तिदं, संवत्सरे मन्त्र-क्रिया-द्रव्य-हीनं यत् कृतं तद्दोष-शान्त्यर्थं पवित्रारोपणं कुर्यात्। पवित्रारोपण-हीना या पूजा सा निष्फला भवति, राज-राष्ट्रं विनाशयति (or विनश्यति)। तस्माद् यत्नेन पवित्रम् आरोपयेत् ॥ sarvayajñaphalapradaṃ, sarvadoṣopaśamanaṃ, sarvatuṣṭikaraṃ, sarvakāmapradaṃ, sarvaloka-śāntidaṃ, saṃvatsare mantra-kriyā-dravya-hīnaṃ yat kṛtaṃ taddoṣa-śāntyarthaṃ pavitrāropaṇaṃ kuryāt | pavitrāropaṇa-hīnā yā pūjā sā niṣphalā bhavati, rāja-rāṣṭraṃ vināśayati (or vinaśyati) | tasmād yatnena pavitram āropayet ||

English Translation

  • Introduction: Now, I shall declare the rules for the offering of the sacred threads (atha pavitrāropaṇaṃ vakṣye).

  • The Merits (Phala): The sacred thread offering (pavitrāropaṇa) yields the fruits of all sacrifices (sarva-yajña-phala-pradaṃ), pacifies all errors (sarva-doṣa-upaśamanaṃ), causes total satisfaction (sarva-tuṣṭikaraṃ), grants all desires (sarva-kāmapradaṃ), and brings peace to all worlds (sarvaloka-śāntidaṃ).

  • Atonement for Omissions: It should be performed to pacify the defects (tad-doṣa-śāntyarthaṃ) of whatever worship was conducted during the year (saṃvatsare) lacking in mantras, rituals, or materials (mantra-kriyā-dravya-hīnaṃ yat kṛtam).

  • Dire Warnings: Worship that is devoid of the sacred thread offering (pavitrāropaṇa-hīnā yā pūjā) becomes entirely fruitless (niṣphalā bhavati), and leads to the destruction of the king and the kingdom (rāja-rāṣṭraṃ vināśayati). Therefore, one should make the offering of the sacred thread with utmost effort (tasmād yatnena pavitram āropayet).

Section 2: Favorable and Forbidden Timings

Verse / Paragraph 1

Sanskrit (Devanagari):

तस्येष्टकालः आषाढ-श्रावण-प्रौष्ठपदेषु मासेषु चतुर्षु सङ्क्रान्तिषु वा केचिद् वदन्ति।

Transliteration (IAST):

tasyeṣṭakālaḥ āṣāḍha-śrāvaṇa-prauṣṭhapadeṣu māseṣu caturṣu saṅkrāntiṣu vā kecid vadanti |

Translation (English):

  • The Auspicious Months: Some authorities declare (kecid vadanti) that the offering should be made in the months of Āṣāḍha (June-July), Śrāvaṇa (July-August), or Prauṣṭhapada/Bhādrapada (August-September), or during the four solar transitions (caturṣu saṅkrāntiṣu vā).

Verse / Paragraph 2

Sanskrit (Devanagari):

शुक्लद्वादश्यां विष्ण्वृक्ष-दिनेषु (श्रवणनक्षत्रे) वा केचिद् आचक्षते।

Transliteration (IAST):

śukladvādaśyāṃ viṣṇvṛkṣa-dineṣu (śravaṇanakṣatre) vā kecid ācakṣate |

Translation (English):

  • The Auspicious Days: Others state (kecid ācakṣate) that it should be performed on the twelfth lunar day of the bright fortnight (śukla-dvādaśyāṃ), or on days ruled by Viṣṇu’s star (viṣṇvṛkṣa-dineṣu, i.e., Śravaṇa nakṣatra).

Verse / Paragraph 3

Sanskrit (Devanagari):

संसर्प-अंहस्पत्य्-अधिमास-तिथि-वार-नक्षत्र-अनुकूलं, व्यतीपातादि-दिनं (मृताहेकादशी-मिश्र-दिनम्‌) वर्जयित्वा, अन्यदोषैर्विना आरोपयेत्।

Transliteration (IAST):

saṃsarpa-aṃhaspaty-adhimāsa-tithi-vāra-nakṣatra-anukūlaṃ, vyatīpātādi-dinaṃ (mṛtāhekādaśī-miśra-dinam) varjayitvā, anyadoṣairvinā āropayet |

Translation (English):

  • Favorable Astrological Conditions: One should perform the offering during times that are favorable (anukūlaṃ) in month, lunar day (tithi), day of the week (vāra), and asterism (nakṣatra), while avoiding intercalary and double-intercalary months (saṃsarpa-aṃhaspaty-adhimāsa), inauspicious astrological combinations such as Vyatīpāta (vyatīpātādi-dinaṃ), and days contaminated by ancestral death anniversaries or improper lunar conjunctions (mṛtāhekādaśī-miśra-dinam).

Verse / Paragraph 4

Sanskrit (Devanagari):

राजाज्ञया चेत् एतेष्वपि पवित्रम् आरोपयेत् ॥

Transliteration (IAST):

rājājñayā cet eteṣvapi pavitram āropayet ||

Translation (English):

  • Royal Dispensation: If ordered by the king (rājājñayā cet), the sacred thread may be offered even during those forbidden times (eteṣvapi pavitram āropayet).

Section 3: Materials and Preparation of the Sacred Threads

Sanskrit (Devanagari) & Transliteration (IAST)

पवित्रारोपणप्रयोगः आलयात् पुरतो मण्डपे, प्राच्यां वा, यागशालां कृत्वा, पादैः षोडशभिः, चतुर्विंशद्भिः वा अन्तस्तम्भविवर्जितं वा, तन्मध्ये द्वादशहस्त-आयत-विस्तारं, तालोच्छ्रायं, मण्डपोच्छ्रायं वा, दर्पणोदरवद् एव कृत्वा, चतुर्दिक्षु तोरण-पूर्णकुम्भ-अङ्कुर-दर्भमाला-तरङ्ग-स्तम्भवेष्टन-मुक्तादामादिभिः अलङ्कृत्य, वेद्याः पुरतः पौण्डरीकम् अग्निकुण्डं सभ्याग्निकुण्डं वा कृत्वा, गोमयेन उपलिप्य, पञ्चवर्णैः अलङ्कृत्य, सम्भारान् आहरेत्। pavitrāropaṇaprayogaḥ ālayāt purato maṇḍape, prācyāṃ vā, yāgaśālāṃ kṛtvā, pādaiḥ ṣoḍaśabhiḥ, caturviṃśadbhir vā antastambhavivarjitaṃ vā, tanmadhye dvādaśahasta-āyata-vistāraṃ, tālocchrāyaṃ, maṇḍapocchrāyaṃ vā, darpaṇodaravad eva kṛtvā, caturdikṣu toraṇa-pūrṇakumbha-aṅkura-darbhamālā-taraṅga-stambhaveṣṭana-muktādāmādibhiḥ alaṅkṛtya, vedyāḥ purataḥ pauṇḍarīkam agnikuṇḍaṃ sabhyāgnikuṇḍaṃ vā kṛtvā, gomayena upalipya, pañcavarṇaiḥ alaṅkṛtya, sambhārān āheret ||

गन्धतोयैः ‘आपो हि ष्ठा’ ‘हिरण्यवर्णा’ ‘पवमानः’ इति प्रोक्ष्य, तद्दिनात् पूर्वे पूर्ववद् अङ्कुरान् अर्पयित्वा, पट्ट-क्षौम-सुवर्ण-सूत्रम् उत्तमम्, मध्यमं क्षौमं (or पद्मं), अधमं कार्पासम्। gandhatoyaiḥ ‘āpo hi ṣṭhā’ ‘hiraṇyavarṇā’ ‘pavamānaḥ’ iti prokṣya, taddināt pūrve pūrvavad aṅkurān arpayitvā, paṭṭa-kṣauma-suvarṇa-sūtram uttamam, madhyamaṃ kṣaumaṃ (or padmaṃ), adhamaṃ kārpāsam ||

सर्वदोष-विवर्जितं, पतिव्रतया सुमङ्गल्या ब्राह्मण्या कन्यकया वा निर्मितं सूत्रं गृह्णीयात्। तदलाभे पण्यविक्रयिणः सकाशाद् (or क्रयाद्), मूषिकादिभिः आघ्रातं, केशादियुक्तं सूत्रं हीनं वर्जयित्वा, सर्वतः समसूत्रं गायत्र्या त्रिगुणीकृत्य, ‘आपो हि ष्ठा’ ‘यन्मे गर्भ’ इति कुशाग्रैः पञ्चगव्येन प्रोक्ष्य, अष्टोत्तरशतं सूत्रं ग्रीवापृष्ठम् आरभ्य जान्वन्तम् उत्तमम्, ऊरुपर्यन्तं (or ऊरु-अन्तं) मध्यमम्, नाभ्यन्तम् अधमम्। sarvadoṣa-vivarjitaṃ, pativratayā sumaṅgalyā brāhmaṇyā kanyakayā vā nirmitaṃ sūtraṃ gṛhṇīyāt | tadalābhe paṇyavikrayiṇaḥ sakāśād (or krayād), mūṣikādibhiḥ āghrātaṃ, keśādiyuktaṃ sūtraṃ hīnaṃ varjayitvā, sarvataḥ samasūtraṃ gāyatriyā triguṇīkṛtya, ‘āpo hi ṣṭhā’ ‘yanme garbha’ iti kuśāgraiḥ pañcagavyena prokṣya, aṣṭottaraśataṃ sūtraṃ grīvāpṛṣṭham ārabhyā jānvantam uttamam, ūruparyantam (or ūru-antam) madhyamam, nābhyantam adhamam ||

विम्ब-आलम्ब-प्रमाणम् अष्टोत्तरसहस्रैः वनमालां कुर्यात्। vimba-ālamba-pramāṇam aṣṭottarasahasraiḥ vanamālāṃ kuryāt ||

ग्रन्थि-अन्तरम् अङ्गुष्ठ-प्रमाणम् उत्तमम्, मध्यमाङ्गुलि-प्रमाणं मध्यमम्, अनामिका-अङ्गुलि-प्रमाणम् अधमम्; ग्रन्थिं विष्णुसूक्तेन बध्नीयात्। granthi-antaram aṅguṣṭha-pramāṇam uttamam, madhyamāṅguli-pramāṇaṃ madhyamam, anāmikā-aṅguli-pramāṇam adhamam; granthiṃ viṣṇusūktena badhnīyāt ||

उपवीतं सप्रणवादि-ग्रन्थि, यजमान-आचार्य-शिष्याणां वह्निकुण्ड-कुम्भानाम् अष्टग्रन्थिं, परिवाराणां द्वादशग्रन्धिं, कुङ्कुम-उशीर-चन्दन-सौवर्ण-राजत-चूर्णैः यथालाभैः एतैः चूर्णैः (or हरिद्रा-चूर्णैः) ग्रन्थीन् अलङ्कृत्य, ‘गन्धद्वारां’ इति अगुरुणा धूपयेत् ॥ upavītaṃ sapraṇavādi-granthi, yajamāna-ācārya-śiṣyāṇāṃ vahnikuṇḍa-kumbhānām aṣṭagranthiṃ, parivārāṇāṃ dvādaśagranthiṃ, kuṅkuma-uśīra-candana-sauvarṇa-rājata-cūrṇaiḥ yathālābhaiḥ etaiḥ cūrṇaiḥ (or haridrā-cūrṇaiḥ) granthīn alaṅkṛtya, ‘gandhadvārāṃ’ iti aguruṇā dhūpayet ||

English Translation

  • The Ritual Hall (Yāgaśālā): In a pavilion situated in front of the temple (ālayāt purato maṇḍape) or to the east, one should erect a sacrificial hall (yāgaśālāṃ kṛtvā) supported by sixteen or twenty-four pillars (pādaiḥ ṣoḍaśabhiḥ caturviṃśadbhir vā), or constructed without internal pillars (antastambha-vivarjitaṃ). In its center, an altar measuring twelve cubits in length and width (dvādaśahasta-āyata-vistāraṃ) should be made, with a height of one tāla or corresponding to the pavilion’s height, polished to be smooth like the surface of a mirror (darpaṇodaravad eva kṛtvā).

  • Decoration: It must be decorated on all four sides with arches, full pots, sprouts, garlands of darbha grass, banners, pillar wraps, and pearl strings (toraṇa-pūrṇakumbha-aṅkura-darbhamālā-taraṅga-stambhaveṣṭana-muktādāmādibhiḥ). In front of the altar, one should construct a Pauṇḍarīka or Sabhya fire pit (pauṇḍarīkam agnikuṇḍaṃ sabhyāgnikuṇḍaṃ vā kṛtvā), smear the area with cow dung, ornament it with five colored powders (pañcavarṇaiḥ alaṅkṛtya), and gather the required sacrificial materials (sambhārān āheret).

  • Thread Materials: Having sprinkled the area with fragrant water reciting the “āpo hi ṣṭhā”, “hiraṇyavarṇā”, and “pavamānaḥ” hymns, and having offered the germinated sprouts on the preceding day as before, one should prepare the threads. Silk, fine linen, or gold thread is best (paṭṭa-kṣauma-suvarṇa-sūtram uttamam); standard linen (or lotus thread) is medium (madhyamaṃ kṣaumaṃ); cotton thread is lowest (adhamaṃ kārpāsam).

  • Purity of Threads: The thread must be free from all flaws (sarvadoṣa-vivarjitaṃ) and spun by a devoted wife, an auspicious woman, a Brahmin woman, or a young maiden (pativratayā sumaṅgalyā brāhmaṇyā kanyakayā vā nirmitaṃ). If unavailable, threads purchased from merchants (paṇyavikrayiṇaḥ sakāśāt) may be used, but one must discard any thread smelled by mice or other animals, containing hair, or otherwise deficient (mūṣikādibhiḥ āghrātaṃ keśādiyuktaṃ sūtraṃ hīnaṃ varjayitvā).

  • Consecration of Lengths: The thread should be uniform throughout (sarvataḥ samasūtraṃ), tripled reciting the Gāyatri mantra (gāyatriyā triguṇīkṛtya), and sprinkled with pañcagavya using the tips of kuśa grass while reciting “āpo hi ṣṭhā” and “yanme garbha”. A thread of 108 strands (aṣṭottara-śataṃ sūtraṃ) is prepared in three lengths:

    1. Best (Uttama): Extending from the back of the neck down to the knees (grīvā-pṛṣṭham ārabhyā jānvantam).
    2. Medium (Madhyama): Extending to the thighs (ūruparyantam).
    3. Lowest (Adhama): Extending to the navel (nābhyantam).

  • The Garland Thread: One should make a forest-garland (vanamālā) composed of 1008 strands (aṣṭottara-sahasraiḥ) equal to the length of the deity’s body/icon (vimba-ālamba-pramāṇam).

  • The Knots (Granthi): The spacing between knots is of three classes:

    1. Best (Uttama): Fleshy width of a thumb (aṅguṣṭha-pramāṇam).
    2. Medium (Madhyama): Length of the middle finger (madhyamāṅguli-pramāṇaṃ).
    3. Lowest (Adhama): Length of the ring finger (anāmikā-aṅguli-pramāṇam).
    • The knots must be tied while reciting the Viṣṇu-sūkta (granthiṃ viṣṇusūktena badhnīyāt).

  • Ritual Use and Anointing: The sacrificial cords (upavītaṃ) should have knots tied with the Praṇava mantra (sapraṇavādi-granthi). For the patron, priest, and disciples, and for the fire pit and pots, it should have eight knots (aṣṭagranthiṃ); for the retinue deities, twelve knots (dvādazagranthiṃ). Anointing the knots with saffron, uśīra grass, sandalwood, gold, and silver powders as available, one should perfume them with aguru incense while reciting “gandhadvārām” (gandhadvārāṃ iti aguruṇā dhūpayet).

Section 4: Consecration of the Bed and Vigil on the Eleventh Day

Sanskrit (Devanagari) & Transliteration (IAST)

पूर्वोक्तान् अङ्कुरान् अर्पयित्वा, आचार्यादिः एकादश्यां प्रभाते स्नात्वा, सन्ध्याम् उपास्य, देवान् (ऋषीन् पितॄंश्च) सन्तर्प्य (or सन्तर्पयित्वा), ब्रह्मजप्यादिं कृत्वा, तद् आलयम् आसाद्य प्रदक्षिणं (कृत्वा), प्राणायामं च कृत्वा, देवाभिमुखे समासीनः ध्यायन् समाहितः अष्टाक्षरं जप्त्वा, आसमाप्तेः उपोष्य, शूद्रान् न अभिभाषेत्। यागशालां प्रविश्य, ‘भगवतो बलेन’ इति देवं प्रार्थयेत्। pūrvoktān aṅkurān arpayitvā, ācāryādiḥ ekādaśyāṃ prabhāte snātvā, sandhyām upāsya, devān (ṛṣīn pitṝṃśca) santarpya (or santarpayitvā), brahmajapyādiṃ kṛtvā, tad ālayam āsādya pradaxiṇaṃ (kṛtvā), prāṇāyāmaṃ ca kṛtvā, devābhimukhe samāsīnaḥ dhyāyan samāhitaḥ aṣṭākṣaraṃ japtvā, āsamāpteḥ upoṣya, śūdrān na abhibhāṣet | yāgaśālāṃ praviśya, ‘bhagavato balena’ iti devaṃ prārthayet ||

English Translation

  • Preparations on the Eleventh Day (Ekādaśī): Having offered the sprouts, the priest and assistants should bathe in the morning of the eleventh day (ekādaśyāṃ prabhāte snātvā), perform sandhyavandana, satisfy the gods, sages, and ancestors (devān santarpya), and complete the silent recitation of Vedic prayers (brahmajapa). Then, reaching the temple, circumambulating it (pradaxiṇaṃ kṛtvā), and performing breath control (prāṇāyāma), he should sit facing the deity (devābhimukhe samāsīnaḥ), focused and composed, muttering the eight-syllable mantra (aṣṭākṣaraṃ japtvā), fasting until the end of the ritual (āsamāpteḥ upoṣya), and avoiding conversation with uninitiated/non-believers (śūdrān na abhibhāṣet). Entering the sacrificial hall (yāgaśālāṃ praviśya), he prays to the Lord, reciting “by the power of Bhagavan” (bhagavato balena).

  • Bathing the Deity and Thread Consecration: He bathes the icon with the twelve-syllable mantra and the hundred-Varuṇa mantras (śatavāruṇādi-mantraiḥ saṃsnāpya), decorates it, offers the primary fire oblations (ādhāraṃ hutvā), and at the end of the āghāra offers Vaishnava oblations ending with the vyahritis (vaiṣṇavaṃ vyāhṛtyantaṃ juhoti). Placing the threads on a grain heap to the west of the altar (vedyāḥ paścime dhānyapīṭhe sūtrāṇi saṃsthāpya), he worships them from water for feet up to all items (pādyādyantam abhyarcya), recites the Puṇyāha purification, and ties the protective cord (pratisaram ābadhya) as before.

  • Preparing the Altar-Bed: On the bed-altar (śayyāvedyāṃ), he spreads a heap of unhusked rice (shali) measured in dronas, half of that of husked rice, and half of that of sesame seeds, one layer above the other (droṇādi-mānaiḥ śālidhānyaṃ tadardha-taṇḍulaṃ tadardha-tilān uparyupari tīrya). He places firm kuśa and darbha grass pointing east or north (sārān kuśān darbhān prāgagrān udagagrān vā), and places the representation of the five weapons (pañcāyudhaiśca vinyasya).

  • Vigil and Recitations: Accompanied by all musical instruments and the ringing of bells, while reciting the Śākuna-sūkta, the priest, alongside the assistants, places the sacred thread on the bed (pavitraṃ śayanam āropya), and consecrates the threads reciting “vaiṣṇavam” (pavitrāṇyadhibhāsya). Covering the bed with fresh cloths, flowers, and unbroken grains, and touching the threads (sūtraṃ spṛṣṭvā), he mutters the Puruṣa-sūkta, Svasti-sūkta (or Śrī-sūkta), Durgā-sūkta, Rātri-sūkta, Sārasvata-sūkta, Viśvajit-sūkta, “ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca”, “sahasraśīrṣam”, Śrī-sūkta, Bhū-sūkta, Viṣṇu-sūkta, Go-sūkta, Ātma-sūkta, and Vaiṣṇava mantras. For the best ritual he recites twelve hymns, seven for the medium, and five for the lowest (uttamaṃ dvādaśa-sūktaṃ, madhyamaṃ sapta, adhamaṃ pañca-sūktaṃ japet).

  • Retinue and Hotar Setup: Next, having worshipped the all-protective wheel (sarvaraxācakraṃ), he worships the deities of the three enclosures around it in clockwise order. He places grass bundles (kūrcān) on the grain heap for the temple guardians like Śaṅkha, Garuḍa, Cakra, and Yakṣas, worships them from water for feet onwards, offers a bali offering consisting of mung-bean rice (mudga-anna-sahitaṃ baliṃ dattvā), washes his feet (svapādau praxālya), and whispers the mantra in his right ear. Lighting the fire (agniṃ prajvālya), he calls the hotar priest, washes his feet, performs achamana, presents him with dakshina along with water, praises the hotar office (hotraṃ praśaṃsya), and invokes the deities of the temple (devamūrtīḥ āvāhayet).

जुष्टाकारं च स्वाहाकारं च कृत्वा, देवेशं शुद्धोदकेन संस्नाप्य, प्लोतेन प्रमृज्य, वस्त्राभरणैः अलङ्कृत्य, पुण्यपुष्पाद्यैः अभ्यर्च्य, सोपवीतानि (consecrated upavitas) पवित्राणि देवम् आरोपयेत्। तदा श्री-भूम्योः, द्वारेन्द्रादि-सर्व-देवानां च आरोपयेत्। juṣṭākāraṃ ca svāhākāraṃ ca kṛtvā, deveśaṃ śuddhodakena saṃsnāpya, plotena pramṛjya, vastrābharaṇaiḥ alaṅkṛtya, puṇyapūṣpādyaiḥ abhyarcya, sopavītāni pavitrāṇi devam āropayet | tadā śrī-bhūmyoḥ, dvārendrādi-sarva-devānāṃ ca āropayet ||

आलयं मण्डपं च विष्णुसूक्तेन पुरुषसूक्तेन च आवेष्ट्य, महाहविः प्रभूतं वा यथाशक्ति निवेद्य, पाद्य-आचमनीयं दत्त्वा, कर्पूर-जातीफल-एला-लवङ्ग-सहितं क्रमुकफल-ताम्बूलं मुखवासं दत्त्वा, बलिं निरवपेद् इति। ālayaṃ maṇḍapaṃ ca viṣṇusūktena puruṣasūktena ca āveṣṭya, mahāhavis prabhūtaṃ vā yathāśakti nivedya, pādya-ācamanīyaṃ dattvā, karpūra-jātīphala-elā-lavaṅga-sahitaṃ kramukaphala-tāmbūlaṃ mukhavāsaṃ dattvā, baliṃ niravaped iti ||

पूर्ववद् यजमान-आचार्य-शिष्याणाम् उपवीतं (or पवित्रं) देव-सन्निधौ दत्त्वा, यागशालां प्रविश्य, अग्निं प्रज्वाल्य परिषिच्य, श्रीसूक्त-महीसूक्त-विष्णुसूक्तैः हुत्वा, सूत्रसंख्यया वैष्णवं प्रत्येकं जुहोति। ओङ्कारादि पारमात्मिकं सर्वदेवत्यं, धात्रादि पञ्चावरणं (or पञ्चवारुणं) मूलहोमं जुहोति। pūrvavad yajamāna-ācārya-śiṣyāṇām upavītaṃ (or pavitraṃ) deva-sannidhau dattvā, yāgaśālāṃ praviśya, agniṃ prajvālya pariṣicya, śrīsūkta-mahīsūkta-viṣṇusūktaiḥ hutvā, sūtrasaṅkhyayā vaiṣṇavaṃ pratyekaṃ juhoti | oṅkārādi pāramātmikaṃ sarvadevatyaṃ, dhātrādi pañcāvaraṇaṃ (or pañcavāruṇaṃ) mūlahomaṃ juhoti ||

जपं कर्तुम् इच्छेत् चेत् — विष्णुसूक्तं, सर्वदेवानां मन्त्रेण (or मन्त्रं च) हुत्वा (or जप्त्वा) एवं यजेत उत्तमम्। कौतुकादि-सर्व-मूर्तीनां पारमात्मिकं सर्वदेवत्यं च जप्त्वा मध्यमम्। अन्यद् उक्तम् अधमम्। japaṃ kartum icchet cet — viṣṇusūktaṃ, sarvadevānāṃ mantreṇa (or mantraṃ ca) hutvā (or japtvā) evaṃ yajeta uttamam | kautukādi-sarva-mūrtīnāṃ pāramātmikaṃ sarvadevatyaṃ ca japtvā madhyamam | anyad uktam adhamam ||

प्रागादि-चतुर्दिक्षु चतुर्वेदान्, पूर्वादि-चतुर्दिक्षु दक्षिण-वामयोः वा, आचार्यः ऋत्विजश्च परात्परतरं गुह्यं सर्ववेदार्थ-सारभूतम् अष्टाक्षरं द्वादशाक्षरं च त्रिषु वह्निषु हुत्वा, नृत्त-गेय-वाद्यैश्च ॥ prāgādi-caturdikṣu caturvedān, pūrvādi-caturdikṣu daxiṇa-vāmayoḥ vā, ācāryaḥ ṛtvijasca parātparataraṃ guhyaṃ sarvavedārtha-sārabhūtam aṣṭākṣaraṃ dvādāśākṣaraṃ ca triṣu vahniṣu hutvā, nṛtta-geya-vādyaiśca ||

English Translation

  • The Offering to the Main Deity: Having performed the Juṣṭākāra and Svāhākāra oblations, he bathes the Lord of Gods with pure water (deveśaṃ śuddhodakena saṃsnāpya), wipes him with a clean cloth (plotena pramṛjya), decorates him with garments and ornaments (vastrābharaṇaiḥ alaṅkṛtya), and worships him with pure flowers and other offerings. Then, he should place the sacred threads along with the upavitas (sopavītāni pavitrāṇi) upon the deity (devam āropayet).

  • Offerings to Retinue Deities: At that time, he should also place them on Śrī and Bhumi (śrī-bhūmyoḥ), and on all the threshold and gate guardian deities (dvārendrādi-sarva-devānāṃ ca āropayet).

  • Temple Wrapping and Food Offering: Wrapping the temple and the pavilion with thread while reciting the Viṣṇu-sūkta and Puruṣa-sūkta (ālayaṃ maṇḍapaṃ ca viṣṇusūktena puruṣasūktena ca āveṣṭya), he offers a grand food offering or a large quantity of food according to his capacity (mahāhavis prabhūtaṃ vā yathāśakti nivedya), presents water for washing feet and sipping, and gives betel leaves with betel nut containing camphor, nutmeg, cardamom, and cloves as mouth-freshener (kramukaphala-tāmbūlaṃ mukhavāsaṃ dattvā). He then distributes the bali offering (baliṃ niravaped iti).

  • Initiates’ Cords and Root Homa: As before, having given the sacred threads to the patron, priest, and disciples in the presence of the deity, he enters the sacrificial hall, lights the fire, sprinkles water around it, offers oblations with the Śrī-sūkta, Mahī-sūkta, and Viṣṇu-sūkta, and offers a Vaiṣṇava oblation for each thread according to the count (sūtrasaṅkhyayā vaiṣṇavaṃ pratyekaṃ juhoti). He performs the root fire offerings dedicated to all deities starting with the Omkara and Paramatmika mantras, and to the deities of the five enclosures starting with Dhata (oṅkārādi pāramātmikaṃ sarvadevatyaṃ dhātrādi pañcāvaraṇaṃ mūlahomaṃ juhoti).

  • Performers’ Japa: If he desires to perform japa (japaṃ kartum icchet cet) — reciting the Viṣṇu-sūkta and the mantras of all deities is best (uttamam). Reciting the Pāramātmika and Sarvadevatya mantras for the Kautuka and other images is medium (madhyamam). Any other recitation is lowest (adhamam).

  • Vedic Recitations and Offerings: The priest and assistants, placing the four Vedas in the four directions starting with the east (prāgādi-caturdikṣu caturvedān), or on the east, south, west, and north, offer in the three fires (triṣu vahniṣu hutvā) the supreme, secret eight-syllable and twelve-syllable mantras representing the essence of all Vedic wisdom, accompanied by dance, song, and musical instruments (nṛtta-geya-vādyaiśca).

Glossary of Key Terms

  1. Pavitrāropaṇa (पवित्रारोपण): The ritual offering of specially woven, purified, and knotted sacred threads to the temple deity to make amends for omissions in the year’s worship.
  2. Yāgaśālā (यागशाला): The ritual pavilion or sacrificial hall constructed near the temple for fire offerings and preparatory purifications.
  3. Vimba-ālamba (विम्बाळम्ब / बिम्बालम्ब): The height/proportion of the deity’s physical icon, used as a reference to determine the length of the sacred threads.
  4. Granthi (ग्रन्थि): The knots tied along the sacred thread representing various energies and mantras.
  5. Aṣṭottara-śata-sūtra (अष्टोत्तरशतसूत्र): A sacred thread bundle containing exactly 108 strands.
  6. Vanamālā (वनमाला): A grand sacred thread or garland of 1008 strands reflecting the supreme garland of Viṣṇu.
  7. Ekādaśy-adhivāsana (एकादश्यधिवासन): The initial purification, consecration, and overnight resting of the threads on the eleventh lunar day.
  8. Dvādaśy-arpaṇa (द्वादश्यर्पण): The actual offering and draping of the consecrated threads on the temple icons on the twelfth lunar day.
  9. Pañcāvaraṇa-homa (पञ्चावरणहोम): Fire offerings dedicated to the deities inhabiting the five concentric enclosures of the Agamic temple layout.
  10. Hotar (होता / होतृ): The priest responsible for reciting the Rigvedic hymns and executing specific offerings during Agamic homas.
